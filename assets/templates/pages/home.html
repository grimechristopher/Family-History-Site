None of this is written down anywhere. What you remember is the only copy, and once it’s gone it’s gone.

It’s for the kids, the grandkids, and their kids after them, so that one day they can read this and know the people they came from. Some of the people in these questions I never met, and some I only know so much about: a name, a couple of stories, and then nothing. I want to know as much as I can, and this is the only way to get it.

{{/* Four blocks rather than four more sentences: this is the part that gets glanced at again later, so the opening words have to carry it. */}}
  • Go on as long as you like. Longer is better. There’s no such thing as too much.
  • Wander off the question. If you start on one thing and remember another, put it all in the same answer. I’d much rather have it than have it tidy.
  • Don’t decide what’s worth keeping. Say it anyway, even if you’re not sure it matters. The small things turn out to be the ones we’d never have known otherwise.
  • Nothing has to be finished. It saves as you go, so you can stop halfway and come back.
{{/* The note says how to write. This says what the site lets you do, which is a different question and worth answering separately -- there is no other place that explains that answers can be replied to, or that you can add a question. */}}

What you can do here

  • Answer a question. Tap a card and start writing. If you’d rather talk than type, use the microphone on the iPad keyboard.
  • Reply to an answer. Any answer can be added to, including each other’s. If you remember it differently, or you know the part that got left out, put it underneath.
  • Write a story with no question. Open a person’s page from the tree and write whatever you like about them.
  • Ask a question of your own. Also on a person’s page. You choose who answers it, yourself or somebody else.
